How DeliverIQ Predicts Delivery Time

DeliverIQ uses a CatBoost machine learning model trained on over 200,000
past orders to predict delivery_days for a new order. The prediction is not
a single fixed number — the app runs multiple simulated samples and returns
a mean, minimum, and maximum estimate to reflect real-world variability.

The most important factors driving the prediction, in order of importance:
1. Route distance (km) between customer and seller — by far the strongest
   factor, based on straight-line (haversine) distance between their
   locations.
2. Express delivery flag — whether the customer selected express shipping.
3. Same-state flag — whether the customer and seller are in the same
   Brazilian state; cross-state shipments generally take longer due to
   inter-state logistics handoffs.
4. Seller's average dispatch time — some sellers historically take longer
   to hand off packages to couriers than others.
5. Estimated delivery days — the platform's own initial estimate at
   checkout, which itself carries information about expected shipping
   difficulty.
6. Road condition — routes marked as "poor" or "damaged" add measurable
   delivery time compared to "good" or "excellent" roads.
7. Traffic index — a 1-10 score representing route congestion; higher
   values are associated with modestly longer delivery times.

Smaller factors include weather, warehouse processing hours, holiday and
festival flags, and courier vehicle breakdown incidents.

Product category, price, and physical product weight/dimensions have only a
small effect on delivery time — they matter more for fragility/handling than
for shipping speed.
